# Garden rooms on Google Ads (UK) — what £4,000 is walking into

**Prepared:** 22 September 2026
**Market:** United Kingdom (Google location code 2826, English)
**Source:** Google Ads Keywords Data + Google Ads Transparency, pulled live via DataForSEO
**Research cost:** $0.362 (~£0.27). Method and itemised spend at the end.

> **On currency.** Google's API returns click prices in **USD**. Every price in this
> report has been converted to GBP at **1 USD = 0.747 GBP** (market rate, 21 Sep 2026)
> and is shown in £. Search volumes are unaffected by this.

---

## The bottom line

There is a real market here — about **227,000 UK searches a month** across the
garden-room/garden-office/garden-studio universe — but it is **not** where the
volume looks like it is, and it is **not** priced like a £25k product.

Three things define what you're walking into:

1. **The head terms are cheap and dirty; the buyer terms are expensive and tiny.**
   `garden room` costs £2.32 a click. `garden rooms surrey` costs £13.77. The
   cheap clicks are people reading; the expensive clicks are people buying.
2. **Google thinks "garden room" means "shed".** Its own expansion of your
   category is **7.4× more shed traffic than garden-room traffic**. Broad match
   will spend your money there.
3. **£4,000 is roughly 1,250 clicks.** That is the whole budget, in one number.
   Everything below is about who those 1,250 people are.

## 1. What £4,000 actually buys

I ran Google's own traffic forecaster twice at a £6 max bid — once on a tight
set of 24 buyer-intent terms on **exact** match, once on 12 category head terms
on **broad** match.

| Campaign shape | Clicks available / month | Avg cost per click | Cost to capture it all | £4,000 lasts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Exact**, 24 buyer terms | 456 | **£3.20** | £1,462/mo | **82 days** |
| **Broad**, 12 head terms | 11,098 | **£3.04** | £33,781/mo | **4 days** |

The trap is in the middle column. **The two shapes cost almost the same per
click** — £3.20 vs £3.04 — so £4,000 buys about 1,250 clicks either way. Broad
match does not make your money go further. It makes it go *faster*, and to
different people: on the exact set you'd own roughly **all** the buyer traffic
in the UK for nearly three months; on broad you'd buy 1,250 clicks drawn from a
pool of 11,098 that Google is free to fill with whatever it considers related.

That £33,781/month figure is the single most useful number here. It is what the
broad category *costs* if you try to cover it. You are bringing 12% of one
month's worth.

**Break-even arithmetic** (not a forecast — see *What I don't know*): a live
competitor ad prices entry-level rooms at *"from £9,485 inc. VAT"*. If your gross
margin on a room of that order is around £3,000, then £4,000 of clicks needs
**two sales** to wash its face — one sale per ~625 clicks, a 0.16% click-to-sale
rate. Whether that is easy or impossible depends entirely on your close rate,
which I have no data on.

## 2. What people are actually searching for — and what they want

This is the part that matters more than the volumes. The searches split into
three populations with completely different readiness to buy, and **Google has
already priced that difference for you**:

| Population | Volume/mo | Avg cost per click | What the low bid tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Researching** (planning, regs, ideas, cost) | 11,270 | **£1.43** | Floor bids as low as **£0.07** |
| **Buying** (near me, builders, companies, county names) | 7,150 | **£8.51** | Floor bids £2.25+ |

**Buyer clicks cost 6× research clicks, and there are fewer of them.**

The tell is not the CPC — it's the *low top-of-page bid*, the cheapest anyone is
paying to appear at the top:

| Keyword | Searches/mo | Cost per click | Low top-of-page bid |
|---|---|---|---|
| do i need planning permission for a garden room | 720 | £1.16 | **£0.07** |
| garden room planning permission | 1,300 | £0.94 | **£0.08** |
| garden room building regulations | 210 | £1.05 | **£0.08** |
| permitted development garden room | 390 | £1.41 | **£0.10** |
| garden room ideas / designs | 3,600 | £1.01 | £0.61 |

Advertisers *are* showing up on these — competition index 84–89 on the planning
terms — but some of them are getting top placement for **seven pence**. That is
a market that has collectively concluded these clicks don't convert. Believe it.

### The intent finding worth acting on

The biggest single research cluster is **planning permission** — 2,620
searches/month across the four phrasings above. These people are not comparing
suppliers. They are trying to find out **whether they are allowed to do this at
all**, before they will let anyone sell to them. It is the gate in front of the
purchase, not a stage of it.

You can see a competitor treating it exactly that way. Green Retreats' live
search ad reads:

> *"Create extra living space and add 5-15% value to your home with a garden
> room. We're the UK's largest luxury garden room company. **No Planning
> Permission.** UK-Leading…"*

They are not bidding on the question — they are **answering it inside an ad on
a commercial term**. That is the right move, and it's available to you for free.

Two other things people are actually trying to do:

- **They're pricing an alternative to building work.** `loft conversion cost`
  (4,400/mo, £3.68), `garage conversion cost` (2,900/mo, £2.38), `single storey
  extension cost` (2,400/mo, £2.04), `house extension cost` (1,900/mo, £3.11).
  That's ~11,600 searches/month of people costing up extra space. Your real
  competitor for the £15k is often a builder, not another garden room firm.
- **They want a named room, not a garden room.** `garden room gym` (1,300/mo),
  `garden room office` (1,300/mo), `home office in garden` (1,300/mo),
  `garden room with bathroom` (590/mo), `garden room with toilet` (590/mo),
  `garden room bar` (480/mo). People search the *use*, then the product.

## 4. The geography trap

Adding a county or city to the search is the strongest buying signal available.
It is also where the auction is most brutal, and the inversion is severe:

| Region | Searches/mo | Avg cost per click |
|---|---|---|
| **SE / commuter belt** (Surrey, Herts, Essex, Kent, London) | 2,250 | **£8.10** |
| **North + Scotland + Wales** (Glasgow, Edinburgh, Manchester, Liverpool, Cardiff, Birmingham, Nottingham, Scotland, Wales) | 3,540 | **£3.13** |

Individually:

| Keyword | Searches/mo | Cost per click | Top-of-page bid range |
|---|---|---|---|
| garden rooms surrey | 480 | **£13.77** | £3.30–£12.77 |
| garden rooms hertfordshire | 140 | £8.93 | £4.37–£16.07 |
| garden rooms essex | 590 | £8.10 | £2.09–£11.46 |
| garden rooms kent | 320 | £6.32 | £1.76–£7.19 |
| garden rooms london | 720 | £4.94 | £1.42–£8.29 |
| garden rooms manchester | 260 | £3.94 | £1.71–£7.57 |
| garden rooms glasgow | 480 | £3.44 | £0.99–£3.44 |
| garden rooms wales | 390 | £2.50 | £0.86–£3.85 |
| garden rooms scotland | 1,300 | £1.91 | £0.84–£2.28 |

**A Surrey click costs 7.2× a Scotland click.** The commuter belt is where the
money is assumed to be, and every garden room firm in the country has bid it to
that conclusion. Scotland has *more* search volume than Surrey (1,300 vs 480) at
a seventh of the price.

The catch: **the entire county layer is only ~7,100 searches/month across 22
regions.** It cannot absorb £4,000. These terms are a scalpel — they will bring
you a few dozen genuinely local, genuinely ready enquiries a month and then run
dry. Plan them as a supplement, not a campaign.

## 5. Google thinks you sell sheds

This is the most expensive mistake available to you, and it's worth being precise
about how I know it.

I seeded Google's own keyword-idea engine with ten garden-room terms — `garden
room`, `garden office`, `garden studio`, `insulated garden room`, `garden pod`
and so on. It returned **3,424 keywords totalling 2.34 million searches/month**.
Of those:

| What came back | Keywords | Searches/mo | Avg cost per click |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual garden rooms / offices / studios | 915 | 227,240 | **£2.31** |
| Sheds, storage, greenhouses, bike stores | 1,339 | **1,678,630** | **£0.72** |

**Google's semantic neighbourhood for your product is 7.4× more shed than garden
room.** `shed` alone is 110,000/mo; `garden shed` 90,500; `plastic shed` 40,500;
`keter shed` 33,100. `summer house` (74,000/mo) and `log cabin` (40,500/mo) are
each as big as or bigger than `garden room` itself.

Two consequences:

- **On broad match, this traffic is what you buy.** It is £0.72 a click, so it
  will make your cost-per-click reports look *excellent* right up until you
  notice nobody has requested a quote. Cheap clicks are the symptom, not the win.
- **Even the head term is contaminated.** `the garden room` (9,900/mo) and `the
  summer house` (22,200/mo) both carry a competition index of **3** — near-zero
  advertiser interest. Those are people looking for venues and restaurants of
  that name, not buildings. A meaningful slice of the 40,500 on `garden room` is
  not a customer under any definition.

Negative keywords are not housekeeping on this account. They are the campaign.

## 6. Who you're actually bidding against

I pulled the live Google ad library for **Green Retreats** (greenretreats.co.uk),
the largest garden-room-specific brand by search demand (`green retreats`,
3,600 searches/mo — people search for them by name).

- **40 creatives currently live** — 26 text, 6 image, 8 video.
- **Their longest-running search ad has been live since 15 February 2022 — 1,675
  days, and it served today.** An ad that survives four and a half years in a
  paid auction is an ad that pays for itself. So is a category that supports one.
- They also run **Google Maps / local ads** carrying a 4.6★ (256 reviews)
  business profile with Call and Directions buttons, and a 4.9★ (106) seller
  rating on their search ads.

Their actual ad copy, read off the rendered creatives:

> **"Fully Insulated Garden Rooms - From £9,485 Inc. Installation"**
> *Garden Rooms from £9,485 incl. VAT - Fully Insulated & Zero Maintenance.*

> **"Recommended By Kevin McCloud - UK's Leading Garden Offices"**
> *Premium insulated garden offices for comfortable year-round working. From
> £9,485. Create a dedicated garden home office, designed and fully installed.*

The repeated, load-bearing elements across four years of surviving ads:
**a specific entry price (£9,485), "inc. VAT", "fully installed", "fully
insulated", "zero maintenance", third-party credibility (Kevin McCloud, star
ratings), and the planning-permission objection pre-answered.**

You are entering an auction where the incumbent leads with a hard number. If
your ads say "bespoke garden rooms, contact us for a quote", you will pay the
same £4.89 for the `garden rooms near me` click and lose it.

And note where the brand demand sits: `dunster house` alone is **49,500
searches/month** — more than `garden room` itself. Excluding it (it's a
multi-product retailer), the named garden-room brands I checked still account
for **16,390 searches/month**. A material share of this category's demand is
already attached to a name, and none of it is yours yet.

## 8. What I'd do with the £4,000

Grounded in the numbers above, not in general practice:

1. **Exact and phrase match only. No broad, not for one day.** Section 5 is why.
   Build the negative list before launch: *shed, sheds, storage, plastic,
   keter, b&q, bike, bicycle, greenhouse, playhouse, summerhouse, log cabin,
   second hand, used, ex display, diy, flat pack, kit, plans, how to build.*
   (`used garden room for sale` is £0.33 a click — that price tells you what
   those searchers intend to spend.)
2. **Anchor the budget on `garden rooms near me`** — 2,400/mo at £4.89, the
   only high-intent term with both volume and a sane price.
3. **Add the county terms for your actual delivery radius**, and check the
   Surrey/Herts/Essex ones against your margin before you turn them on. At
   £13.77 a Surrey click, 100 clicks is £1,377 — a third of the budget for one
   county.
4. **Test `garden studio` (14,800/mo, £2.84, competition 68)** as the one
   volume play. It is the least contested head term in the category.
5. **Put the entry price in the headline.** The incumbent has run "from £9,485
   inc. VAT" for years. Silence on price against a competitor who states one
   costs you the click you already paid for.
6. **Answer planning permission in the ad copy, don't bid on it.** 2,620
   searches/month of planning questions, with top-of-page available at £0.07 —
   a price that means the clicks don't convert. Use the objection, skip the
   keyword.
7. **Spend it in roughly 8–10 weeks, not 6 months.** £400–500/week through
   the autumn peak, hard stop before December. That's also about the window
   where the exact-match set (£1,462/mo at full coverage) can absorb the money
   without you having to loosen targeting to spend it.

## What I don't know, and what I checked but couldn't confirm

Stated plainly, because some of these change the conclusions:

- **No conversion data whatsoever.** I have not measured enquiry rate, close
  rate, lead quality or sales cycle. Every click price here is real; every
  statement about what a click is *worth* is arithmetic on assumptions. The
  break-even figure in section 1 assumes a £3,000 gross margin I invented for
  illustration.
- **Volumes are Google's rounded buckets.** 40,500 / 18,100 / 14,800 are
  Keyword Planner's standard buckets, not counts. Treat them as orders of
  magnitude.
- **Google merges near-duplicate keywords, and I caught it doing so.**
  `garden room insulation` and `insulated garden rooms` came back with
  *identical* figures (6,600/mo, £3.23, same bid range) — as did `garden room
  ideas`/`garden room designs` (3,600) and `garden room suppliers`/`garden room
  manufacturers` (90). Those are cluster totals, not per-term volumes.
- **Two counties I expected came back at zero and I could not confirm why.**
  `garden rooms sussex` and `garden rooms hampshire` both returned no volume —
  yet `garden offices sussex` shows 40/mo at £9.58 a click in the same dataset.
  That means sub-reporting-threshold, not absent demand. Don't read those two as
  dead markets.
- **CPC is a national average, and yours will be worse at first.** These figures
  don't account for Quality Score, ad rank, device, time of day, or the fact
  that a brand-new account with no history typically pays above the quoted rate
  for the first few weeks. Budget for that.
- **The forecast is Google's model, not a promise.** The 456 clicks / £1,462
  figures assume a £6 bid and a competitive Quality Score. It returned no
  impression or CTR data, so I cannot tell you the impression share behind it.
- **I mapped one competitor's ads, not the auction.** Green Retreats was chosen
  as the largest garden-room-specific brand by search demand. I do not know how
  many advertisers are in the auction, what they spend, or how the rest position
  themselves. The £9,485 anchor is one firm's, not the market's floor.
- **No YoY comparison is possible.** The trend data covers Sep 2025–Aug 2026 —
  twelve consecutive months with no repeated month, so there is no like-for-like
  year-on-year figure. I can describe the season; I cannot tell you whether the
  category is growing.
- **I did not look at the organic SERP, Performance Max, Shopping or Local
  Services Ads.** How much of the results page is already taken by
  non-search-ad inventory is unmeasured, and it affects what your text ads can win.
- **FX risk on every price.** Google returned USD; I converted at 0.747. A 5%
  move in sterling moves every £ figure here by 5%. The £9,485 in the competitor
  ad is the one price in this report quoted natively in pounds.
