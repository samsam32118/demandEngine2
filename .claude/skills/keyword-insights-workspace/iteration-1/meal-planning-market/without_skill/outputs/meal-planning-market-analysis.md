# The Meal Planning Market: Graveyard or Opening?

**Prepared:** 22 September 2026 · **Geography:** United States, English
**Evidence base:** 24,028 live Google Ads keywords (Sept 2025 – Aug 2026); 142 keywords with 48 months of history (Sept 2022 – Aug 2026); top 100 Apple App Store results for "meal planner"; 100 most-recent reviews of the category leader; two Google Ads traffic forecasts; two Google SERP pulls for shutdown and funding events.

---

## The short answer

**It is a graveyard and an opening at the same time, and that is the whole finding.**

Four of the best-known meal planning products died or were absorbed in the last 24 months. Over the same period, generic search demand for "meal planning app" grew **+122%** and inflected sharply upward in 2025 — the exact window in which those products were dying.

Demand did not leave. Supply left. The incumbents did not fail because nobody wanted the product; they failed because nobody worked out how to charge enough for it.

That means the question to answer before we build is **not** "is there demand?" (there is, and it is measurably growing). It is **"what do we charge, and who pays it?"** — because that is the specific thing every dead company in this category got wrong.

## 1. The graveyard is real, and it is documented

| Product | Status | Our brand-search data (4-yr change) |
|---|---|---|
| **Yummly** (Whirlpool) | Shut down 20 Dec 2024 — Whirlpool closed its software division | **−51%** (14,092 → 6,850/mo) |
| **PlateJoy** | Discontinued 1 July 2025 — confirmed on PlateJoy's own support page | **−77%** (1,742 → 392/mo) |
| **Whisk** | Absorbed into Samsung Food | **−62%** (1,350 → 512/mo); "samsung food" **+1,489%** |
| **Real Plans** | Still listed, demand collapsed | **−55%** (988 → 449/mo) |
| **Forks Meal Planner** | Closing (reported) | — |

The pure-play meal-planning brand cluster as a whole is down **−26%** over four years (86,201 → 63,434 searches/mo).

**Worth noting for credibility:** our keyword data independently predicted every one of these outcomes. Each product's brand search was collapsing well before the shutdown notice. That is the single most useful diagnostic we have, and we should run it on ourselves quarterly.

### The adjacent commerce category is dying faster

Meal-kit delivery — the category that actually monetised meal planning intent — is down **−19%** in four years, and the decline is concentrated in the biggest names:

| Brand | 2022-23 avg/mo | 2025-26 avg/mo | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| EveryPlate | 169,833 | 46,725 | **−72%** |
| Dinnerly | 43,617 | 12,900 | **−70%** |
| Green Chef | 87,583 | 40,825 | **−53%** |
| HelloFresh | 320,917 | 164,375 | **−49%** |
| Blue Apron | 150,500 | 87,708 | **−42%** |
| Home Chef | 250,333 | 164,000 | **−34%** |
| *CookUnity* | *60,000* | *226,667* | ***+278%*** |
| *Factor* | *389,667* | *425,333* | *+9%* |

The winners are the ones that stopped selling *ingredients plus a recipe* (a planning product) and started selling *finished food* (a delivery product). **The market is paying to not plan, not to plan better.** That is an uncomfortable fact for anyone building a planning tool and we should sit with it.

### The App Store is littered with corpses-in-waiting

Of the **top 100 apps ranking for "meal planner" on the US App Store**:

- **82 have fewer than 10 ratings.** Not 10,000 — ten.
- Only **9** have 10,000+ ratings.
- The **top 3 hold 88.8%** of all ratings in the ranked set.
- Every single one is priced **Free** (freemium/subscription); only two paid apps appear at all, at $1.99 and $2.99.

Barriers to entry are zero, so roughly one new AI meal planner ships per week and essentially none of them reach escape velocity. If we build, we need a reason we are not app #83.

## 2. Why it is not a graveyard: demand grew while the incumbents died

This is the finding I would lead the pitch with.

**"Meal planning app" — quarterly average monthly searches, US:**

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | — | 8,933 | 8,933 | 9,533 | **21,100** |
| Q2 | — | 6,600 | 6,200 | 10,633 | 13,900 |
| Q3 | 6,600 | 8,100 | 6,200 | **20,833** | 13,450 |
| Q4 | 6,700 | 7,100 | 6,700 | 14,800 | — |

Flat at ~6,500–8,900 for **ten straight quarters**, then a step-change from 2025-Q1 onward. Four-year change: **7,458 → 16,542/mo, +122%.**

The whole generic-software-intent cluster moved with it:

| Cluster (4-yr avg monthly searches) | 2022-23 | 2025-26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Generic meal-planning software intent** | 30,798 | 51,133 | **+66%** |
| — "meal planning app" / "meal planner app" | 7,458 | 16,542 | **+122%** |
| — "recipe manager app" | 84 | 1,347 | **+1,500%** |
| — "recipe organizer app" | 472 | 1,513 | **+221%** |
| — "meal planning software" | 494 | 1,093 | **+121%** |
| **B2B / professional software** | 16,969 | 28,931 | **+70%** |
| — "practice better" (dietitian platform) | 11,567 | 24,650 | **+113%** |
| **Paid meal-plan services** | 10,223 | 28,698 | **+181%** |
| — "meal planning service" | 1,883 | 20,658 | **+997%** |
| **AI-native entry terms** | 2,558 | 3,050 | +19% |
| **Pure-play app brands** | 86,201 | 63,434 | **−26%** |
| **Meal-kit / delivery** | 1,682,258 | 1,362,375 | **−19%** |

Read that table as one sentence: **people are searching for the category and not for any company in it.** That is the signature of an unclaimed market, and it is the opposite of what a dead market looks like (dead markets lose category demand first, brand demand last).

### Total addressable search

The meal planning and prep search universe in the US is **7,572 keywords / 4.11M searches per month**. For context on structure:

| | Share of the 4.11M |
|---|---|
| Top 10 keywords | 26.3% |
| Top 50 | 46.4% |
| Top 100 | 57.6% |
| **Top 500** | **82.3%** |
| Long tail (rank 500+) | 17.7% |

This is an unusually **head-heavy** market. That cuts both ways: winning SEO here means winning a few hundred contestable terms rather than grinding out ten thousand — but those few hundred terms are held by AllRecipes, NYT Cooking and the food blogs, not by software companies.

## 3. The money: where it actually is, and where it is not

Volume-weighted CPC by cluster across the full 24,028-keyword corpus:

| Cluster | Keywords | Searches/mo | Vol-wtd CPC | What that means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meal kits / delivery | 1,641 | 1,466,290 | **$15.52** | Real commercial value per click |
| Planning core (software) | 5,277 | 2,181,870 | **$3.35** | Modest |
| Diet / weight-loss programs | 4,436 | 5,082,390 | $2.55 | Modest |
| Recipe content | 3,147 | 15,124,610 | **$0.32** | Effectively worthless per click |

**The tell:** the single most contested planning keyword is the generic head term itself. "meal planning" / "meal planner" / "meal plan" (Google reports these combined) sits at **40,500 searches/mo, $12.84 CPC, competition index 100 out of 100.** Nobody is paying $12.84 to sell a $40/year app. That bid is coming from meal-kit and weight-loss advertisers buying planning intent as a lead source.

**So there is a proven buyer for meal-planning attention — it just isn't the meal planner.** The only business that has ever monetised this intent at scale is arbitrage into commerce, and that commerce pool is shrinking 19%.

Meanwhile, the software term we would actually want to own is cheap and quiet:

> **"meal planning app" — 18,100 searches/mo, $5.15 CPC, competition index 25 (LOW).**

Real, growing volume, and nobody is fighting for it. That is either an opportunity or a verdict on how little the traffic is worth. Section 4 settles which.

### Where CPCs are genuinely high

| Keyword | Searches/mo | CPC |
|---|---|---|

**Professional and service-delivery terms clear at 3–5x the consumer app terms.** The market prices a dietitian's workflow far above a consumer's grocery list.

## 4. Unit economics: can we buy a customer?

We ran live Google Ads traffic forecasts on both segments (exact match, ceiling bid, next month, US).

| | Consumer app cluster (20 terms) | B2B professional cluster (18 terms) |
|---|---|---|
| Available clicks/month | **311** | **127** |
| Average CPC | **$9.86** | **$34.19** |
| Total monthly spend available | $3,069 | $4,347 |

### Finding 4a — Paid search cannot build this company, in either segment

The **entire** exact-match paid-search channel for consumer meal planning in the United States is **311 clicks a month**. Across both segments combined it is roughly **440 clicks/month and under $7,500/month of inventory.** There is no version of "we'll scale on Google" here. Anyone who pitches us a paid-acquisition growth model for this category has not looked at the inventory.

### Finding 4b — Consumer economics are underwater at the observed price point

The category leader prices at **$39.99/year** (taken from its own users' reviews). After Apple's cut, call it **~$28 net in year one**, maybe **~$84 net over three years** at optimistic retention.

At $9.86 per click, the required **click-to-paying-subscriber** conversion is:

| Net LTV assumption | Conversion needed to break even | Conversion needed for 3:1 LTV/CAC |
|---|---|---|
| $28 (1 year) | **35%** | 106% (impossible) |
| $84 (3 years) | **11.7%** | **35%** |

A click-to-paid-subscriber rate of 11.7% is not achievable for a consumer app; realistic is 1–3%. **Paid consumer acquisition is roughly 4–10x underwater at a $40/year price.** The only way consumer works is with a near-zero-CAC channel — organic search, TikTok/Reels, or word of mouth.

### Finding 4c — B2B economics work, but the channel is small

A dietitian/nutrition-coach tool at ~$59/month with 70% annual retention is roughly **$1,400 net LTV.**

| Click→customer rate | CAC at $34.19/click | LTV/CAC |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | $1,710 | 0.8 (bad) |
| 3% | $1,140 | 1.2 (marginal) |
| **5%** | **$684** | **2.0 (workable)** |
| 7% | $488 | 2.9 (good) |

B2B search is **10–30x more forgiving** than consumer because the LTV is 10–30x higher, and 3–7% click-to-customer is normal for high-intent B2B software searches. But 127 clicks/month at 5% is ~6 customers/month — **~$50K of new ARR per year from paid search.** Real, profitable, and not a venture-scale channel on its own.

## 5. What the winner's users actually say

The clearest read on the business-model problem comes from the category leader's own reviews.

**ReciMe** is the standout consumer success: **295,500 ratings at 4.79 stars**, versus Mealime's 54,053 and Plan to Eat's 6,107. It went from nothing to category leader on a **$1.5M seed round** (investors included Marissa Mayer). That is the good news — this market *can* still be won from a standing start, recently, on very little money.

The bad news is in the **100 most recent reviews**:

| | Count |
|---|---|
| 1-star | 35 |
| 2-star | 6 |
| 3-star | 4 |
| 4-star | 7 |
| 5-star | 48 |
| **1–2 star share of most-recent 100** | **41%** |

A 4.79 lifetime average with 41% recent reviews at 1–2 stars means satisfaction is deteriorating fast. And the complaints are almost entirely about **one thing**:

| Theme | Mentions in 100 reviews | Of which 1–2 star |
|---|---|---|
| **Paywall / forced trial** | **32** | **26** |
| Import / recipe capture broken | 21 | 9 |
| Grocery list & planning gaps | 9 | 2 |
| Accuracy / AI errors | 5 | 3 |
| Crashes / lost data | 3 | 2 |

Representative verbatims:

> *"They claim it's free but you have to sign up for a free trial first. If you just want to try the free version you cannot."*
> *"Not remotely free — might be a great app, but pretending like it's free, when it's really $40/yr seems quite disingenuous."*
> *"I would buy this but not sign up for a perpetual subscription."*
> *"Charged $43 out of the blue."*

**Two conclusions.**

1. **The ARPU ceiling is so low that the leader is strip-mining its own goodwill to hit it.** Aggressive paywalling at $40/year is what you do when you cannot charge more. This is the same wall Yummly and PlateJoy hit.
2. **The actual job ReciMe wins on is recipe capture from TikTok/Instagram videos, not meal planning.** Twenty-one of 100 reviews are about import working or failing. Users are hiring it to *save a recipe they saw on social*, and planning is the afterthought. If we build a *planner*, we may be building the feature nobody is actually paying for.

## 6. Where the capital has gone

| Company | Raised | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **Season Health** | **$34M Series A (a16z)**, plus $11M seed | Meal planning + food delivery, **reimbursed through healthcare** |
| Meez | $11.5M (+ $6.5M seed) | Recipe system for **professional kitchens** |
| **ReciMe** | **$1.5M seed** | The #1 **consumer** meal planning app |
| Category total | 1,960 companies, 208 funded, **$726M** all-time (Tracxn, recipe discovery) | — |

The pattern is unambiguous: **institutional money at scale has gone to the payer-funded and professional versions of this problem. The consumer version raises seed-scale cheques and stays seed-scale.** The #1 consumer app in the category raised $1.5M. That is either a warning about the ceiling, or evidence that nobody has properly funded a run at it yet — but we should assume the former until we can argue otherwise.

## 7. Four ways in, ranked

### D. Consumer meal planner (generic) — do not do this
Demand is growing (+122%) and the head term is uncontested (competition index 25). But paid acquisition is 4–10x underwater at $40/year, 82% of App Store entrants have under 10 ratings, and four well-funded predecessors just died here. **Only viable with a genuinely zero-CAC organic channel we can prove before writing code.**

### C. Consumer recipe-capture-first — crowded, and the winner is already there
This is the job users actually pay for, but ReciMe holds 295,500 ratings and Tasty holds 432,783. The monetisation ceiling is visibly $40/year. **Enter only with a structurally different price model** — the reviews contain a direct product brief: *"I would buy this but not sign up for a perpetual subscription."* A one-time-purchase competitor to a subscription leader with 41% recent 1–2 star reviews is a genuine, if modest, opening.

### B. B2B — dietitians, nutrition coaches, gyms — best risk-adjusted start
CPCs of **$22–$34** versus $5.15 consumer. Practice Better's brand search is **+113%** to 24,650/mo, so the category is growing and consolidating around few players. Unit economics work at 5% conversion. **The catch is the ceiling:** category search is tiny (50–170/mo for "nutrition coaching software"), so this is a sales-led, not search-led, business, and paid search adds maybe $50K ARR/year. Good business, not obviously a venture business.

### A. Payer / employer-funded ("food as medicine") — highest ceiling, hardest start
Where the $34M went. Removes the $40/year ARPU ceiling entirely by changing who pays. **Not a search-demand business**, so nothing in this analysis validates or invalidates it — it would need a completely different diligence pass (payer contracts, clinical outcomes, reimbursement codes).

## 8. What I would tell you to do next

1. **Do not build a consumer meal planner without first proving a zero-CAC channel.** Spend two weeks, not two months: run organic TikTok/Reels content against the recipe-capture job and measure saves-to-installs. If that does not work, the economics in section 4 say nothing else will.
2. **Kill the "go niche medical" idea before it costs us time.** We tested it: the constraint-driven cluster (diabetic, renal, PCOS, IBS, bariatric, pregnancy, ADHD, seniors, halal/kosher — 78 keywords, 235,570 searches/mo) is **down −11%** over four years, at a weak $2.31 volume-weighted CPC. The exceptions worth a second look are narrow: **low FODMAP +285%** (1,767 → 6,808/mo, $4.74 CPC, low competition), renal +30%, seniors +53%. Everything else in that cluster is shrinking.
3. **Run the diligence call on the B2B path.** Five conversations with dietitians on what Practice Better does not do. That is the only segment where the arithmetic already closes.
4. **Track brand-search decay as our own early-warning metric.** It called Yummly, PlateJoy, Whisk and Real Plans before the press releases did.

## Appendix: methodology, limits and cost

**Sources.** Google Ads Keywords Data API via DataForSEO (4 calls): one 20-seed expansion returning 24,028 keywords with 12 months of monthly history; one 142-keyword pull with 48 months of history (Sept 2022 – Aug 2026); two exact-match traffic forecasts at ceiling bid. Apple App Store data via DataForSEO (2 calls): top 100 ranked apps for "meal planner", and the 100 most-recent reviews for ReciMe (app id 1593779280). Google SERP via Bright Data (2 calls) for shutdown and funding events.

**Known limits — please read these before quoting any number.**

- **Google combines near-duplicate keywords.** "meal planning", "meal planner" and "meal plan" return one combined 40,500 figure, not three. Cluster totals are therefore indicative, not additive.
- **Trend comparisons** are the mean of the most recent 12 months versus the mean of the earliest 12 months in the 48-month window. This controls for seasonality but is not a formal CAGR.
- **Calendar-year totals for 2022 and 2026 are partial** (Sept–Dec 2022; Jan–Aug 2026) and are not compared directly anywhere in this document.
- **Volume x CPC figures** are upper-bound auction-value indices assuming every search produced a paid click. They are for comparing clusters against each other, not estimates of real ad spend.
- **Traffic forecasts are exact-match only**, which understates total reachable paid inventory — broad match would be larger and lower-intent. The directional conclusion (paid search cannot carry this category) holds under any match type given the underlying volumes.
- **App Store review sentiment** is 100 most-recent reviews for one app. It is a strong signal about that app's monetisation, not a statistical sample of the category.
- **Geography is US/English only.** Metrics swing enormously by country; nothing here says anything about UK, EU or AU markets.
- **LTV assumptions in section 4 are ours, not measured.** The $39.99/year price point is sourced from user reviews; retention and margin assumptions are stated inline and should be challenged.

**Total data cost of this analysis: $0.365** (DataForSEO), plus 2 Bright Data SERP queries.
